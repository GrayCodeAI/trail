# Trail design-partner brief

**Status:** internal recruitment brief; customize before sharing externally

## Proposed pilot

Trail is testing whether explicit outcomes, bounded authority, execution evidence, and human acceptance reduce coordination failure for teams using several coding agents.

The pilot follows one real cross-repository software outcome through the team's existing tools. It does not require replacing the current tracker, Git forge, coding agent, or CI system.

## Good design-partner fit

A team is a strong fit when it:

- uses coding agents every week on maintained or production software;
- operates several agent runs or worktrees concurrently;
- coordinates work across repositories, services, or teams;
- has experienced duplicate work, context loss, unclear authority, difficult resumption, or false completion;
- can name the person who reviews and accepts an outcome;
- can provide a redacted real workflow and comparison baseline;
- will attend a setup, observation, and debrief session;
- can use a manual or disposable prototype without expecting production guarantees.

Initial pilots should exclude employment decisions, healthcare, credit, biometrics, education admissions, law enforcement, critical infrastructure control, and other high-risk domains.

## Partner contribution

The partner provides:

- one primary operator and one acceptance owner;
- one real bounded outcome with non-sensitive or redacted context;
- access to existing workflow artifacts under an agreed scope;
- a comparable recent baseline workflow when possible;
- time for setup, observation, interruption/recovery exercise, and debrief;
- honest permission to publish anonymized aggregate findings, if separately agreed.

The partner does not need to provide production credentials, proprietary source, customer data, or unrestricted system access.

## GraycodeAI contribution

GraycodeAI provides:

- workflow mapping and a written baseline;
- the concierge Trail prototype;
- structured outcome, commitment, authority, evidence, and acceptance records;
- manual/read-only collection of approved evidence;
- a resumption capsule after an injected or natural interruption;
- pilot metrics and a findings report;
- deletion or return of research artifacts according to the agreed retention plan.

## Pilot boundary

- Existing tools remain authoritative for code, repositories, CI, and agent execution.
- Trail does not autonomously dispatch agents or perform production side effects.
- Evidence is referenced or redacted; secrets and proprietary content are excluded.
- A human identified by the partner remains the acceptance owner.
- The prototype may be incomplete, manual, or discarded after the experiment.
- No production availability, security certification, or regulatory-compliance claim is made.

## Success criteria

Before the pilot, both parties choose at least one primary measure and its threshold:

- faster resumption after interruption;
- less duplicate/conflicting work;
- shorter time to identify the next authorized action;
- fewer outputs incorrectly treated as accepted;
- lower review effort for the same confidence;
- better reconstruction of authority, decisions, evidence, and exact accepted version;
- reduced human attention or compute cost per accepted outcome.

Repeat use on a second workflow is the strongest success signal.

## Go/no-go review

Proceed only when:

- the workflow and acceptance owner are real;
- the data boundary is documented;
- baseline evidence can be collected;
- the partner understands the prototype status;
- the chosen metric can change independently of subjective satisfaction;
- withdrawal and artifact deletion can be honored.
